How to calculate your net worth

The total is the starting point. What it depends on is the part worth reviewing.

Use the calculator

Free. Runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent or stored.

Most investors know roughly what their brokerage account is worth. Fewer have a single, current figure for everything they own minus everything they owe, across brokers, exchanges, wallets, property and cash. That figure is your net worth, and calculating it takes four steps.

Net worth = total assets − total liabilities

Step 1. List everything you own

Group your assets by type so nothing is counted twice or forgotten:

  • Cash: current and savings accounts, deposits, cash held at brokers.
  • Listed investments: stocks, ETFs and funds, across every account.
  • Crypto: holdings on exchanges and in self-custody wallets.
  • Precious metals: physical or held through a provider.
  • Real estate: property you own, at a realistic market value.
  • Other assets: anything else with a value you could reasonably realise.

Step 2. Value each asset consistently

Use the same date and the same currency for everything. Listed assets have a market price; for property and other illiquid assets, a conservative estimate is more useful than an optimistic one. If you hold assets in several currencies, convert them all at the same rate and date.

Step 3. List what you owe

Include the outstanding balance of mortgages, personal and car loans, credit card balances and any margin or other borrowing. Use what is owed today, not the original amount.

Step 4. Subtract and record the date

Subtract total liabilities from total assets. Write the result down with its date: a single figure tells you where you stand, while a series of dated figures shows how your wealth evolves.

Beyond the total: what your net worth depends on

Two investors can have the same net worth and very different situations. Once you have the figure, three readings tell you much more than the total alone:

1. How it is split

What share sits in listed investments, crypto, property, metals and cash. A split you chose deliberately years ago can drift as some assets grow faster than others.

2. How concentrated it is

Add up your three largest positions and compare them with the rest. For example, if Microsoft is 29%, Bitcoin 22% and Apple 18% of a portfolio, the three together hold 69%, more than everything else combined. Concentration is not automatically a mistake, but it is worth knowing it is there.

3. How much is available

Cash and other liquid assets determine how much room you have for unexpected expenses or new opportunities without selling something else.

How often to recalculate

There is no fixed rule. Many investors update the figure monthly or quarterly, and after any large move such as a property purchase or a big sale. What matters most is consistency: the same method, the same currency and a date on every figure, so changes reflect your wealth and not your method.

Keep the figure, and the reading, up to date.
